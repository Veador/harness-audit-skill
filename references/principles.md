# Audit Principles

Apply these principles to the target's purpose and actual responsibilities. Assess the combined effect of applicable instructions. Specific mechanisms are needed only where the workflow calls for them.

Apply them to the auditor as well. Its objective and access boundary must be clear; its conclusions must rest on inspected evidence; its recommendations must respect the same constraints. A limitation discovered during the audit must change the affected claim, not merely appear in a disclaimer. The required self-check in SKILL.md applies these principles to the audit's own work.

## Understanding and intent

The work has an identifiable objective, scope, constraints, and basis for acceptance. Important assumptions and unknowns are visible. Instructions support explaining consequential decisions and the relevant system behavior at a level the user can assess. Requiring understanding does not mean exposing hidden reasoning or demanding exhaustive explanations of routine work.

## Relevant context

The agent can identify authoritative inputs, applicable rules, and information needed for the task within authorized access and disclosure boundaries. Missing, conflicting, and outdated information can be surfaced. Source material remains distinguishable from generated interpretations. More context is useful when it improves the decision; accumulating unrelated or stale content is not an improvement by itself. Needing evidence does not authorize obtaining or exposing restricted material.

## Evidence for outcomes

Acceptance rests on the resulting artifact, observed actions, supported facts, or criteria appropriate to the task. An agent's success statement, another agent's agreement, or successful execution of a tool does not establish every requested outcome. Evidence supports claims only within its coverage. Inspection can establish that a rule exists; it cannot establish that the rule was followed.

For open-ended work, assess whether constraints, tradeoffs, and the basis for human judgment are visible. Do not impose objective correctness, external citations, executable tests, or deterministic verdicts where those would not fit the task.

## Valid work and calibrated confidence

Instructions allow the agent to distinguish completed work, a demonstrated problem, and insufficient grounds for a conclusion. Missing information or unperformed checks do not become evidence of success. Conditions and coverage remain explicit.

When another AI carries out a test or other required procedure, check whether the intended procedure actually occurred before interpreting its outcome. When outputs vary, assess whether reliability claims are supported by representative observations; one favorable result or repeated agreement is not a general guarantee.

## Verification of the verifier

When a workflow itself judges outcomes, look for a basis for trusting that judgment. Known acceptable, unacceptable, and insufficient-evidence inputs can help establish the boundaries of a verifier where those categories apply. An independent check must add a useful evidence source or method; another response based only on the same unsupported assertion adds little.

Do not require a fixed number of test cases, a particular evaluator, or a separate reviewer for every task. Assess whether the chosen checks can reveal relevant errors rather than merely repeat the output's assumptions.

## Learning that improves future work

For recurring workflows, confirmed findings can become scoped guidance, reusable evidence, or improved context. Preserve the basis and applicability of a lesson, distinguish hypotheses from established knowledge, and keep outdated guidance revisable. One incident does not justify an unconditional rule for unrelated tasks.

Persistent recording is appropriate when useful and permitted. A bounded task does not automatically need a memory system, new files, or changes to shared instructions.

## Human responsibility and useful autonomy

People retain meaningful control over goals, acceptance, consequential tradeoffs, and authorized effects. Outputs explain what was done, what supports the conclusion, and what remains uncertain well enough for an informed decision. Human approval alone does not establish correctness.

Routine work can proceed within established authority. A sound workflow asks for missing information or decisions when they materially affect the outcome; it does not replace understanding with repeated confirmation or require the user to relay unexplained instructions between agents.
